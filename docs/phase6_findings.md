# Phase 6 — Customer Analytics: Key Findings

Full query-by-query results are in
[`reports/phase6_customer_analytics_report.md`](../reports/phase6_customer_analytics_report.md),
generated by running [`sql/06_customer_analytics.sql`](../sql/06_customer_analytics.sql).
As in Phase 5, findings are labeled **Fact** (directly computed) or
**Observation** (a pattern noticed in the facts). Recommendations are
deferred to Phase 13.

## Customer Base Coverage

* **Fact:** Of 15,266 customers in the master data, **11,887 (77.87%)**
  have placed at least one order; **3,379 (22.13%) have never purchased**.
  This project's customer-behavior metrics (repeat rate, RFM, cohorts) are
  scoped to the 11,887 purchasing customers unless stated otherwise.

## Repeat Purchase Behavior

* **Fact:** **61.18%** of purchasing customers are repeat customers (2+
  distinct orders); 38.82% are one-time buyers.
* **Fact:** Repeat customers generate **82.39%** of total revenue
  ($45.94M) from 61.18% of customers, vs. one-time customers' 17.61%
  ($9.82M) from 38.82% of customers. Average revenue per repeat customer
  ($6,316.84) is **nearly 3x** that of a one-time customer ($2,127.71).
* **Observation:** Revenue concentration among repeat customers is driven
  by both higher order counts *and* higher average spend per customer —
  repeat customers are not simply placing many small orders.
* **Fact:** Purchase frequency drops off sharply and smoothly: 38.82% of
  customers order exactly once, 28.69% exactly twice, 16.85% exactly three
  times, tapering to a maximum observed frequency of 14 orders (1
  customer). No customer has an anomalously high order count relative to
  this distribution.

## Revenue Concentration

* **Fact:** Revenue is concentrated but not extremely so: the top decile
  (highest-spending 10% of purchasing customers, 1,189 people) accounts
  for **35.98%** of total revenue. The top 3 deciles (30% of customers)
  account for **69.07%** of revenue. This is a moderate concentration
  pattern — well short of an extreme "1% of customers = most of revenue"
  scenario, but still meaningfully top-heavy: the bottom half of customers
  (deciles 6–10) contribute only **13.36%** of revenue combined.
* **Fact:** The single highest-spending customer (Matthew Flemming,
  United States) generated $61,871.70 across 9 orders — about 0.11% of
  total revenue. No individual customer dominates; concentration is
  spread across roughly a thousand mid-to-high spenders, not a handful of
  outliers.

## Customer Acquisition Over Time

* **Fact:** New-customer acquisition (by first-order year) peaked in 2018
  (3,104 new customers) and declined in 2019 (2,832) and sharply in 2020
  (947) — consistent with the broader 2020 revenue decline seen in Phase
  5. 2021 (67 new customers) is partial-year only.

## Customer Segments by Country

* **Fact:** United States customers (5,706 people, 53.58% of revenue)
  also have the highest average orders per customer (2.49) and average
  revenue per customer ($5,235.13) among the 4 largest markets.
* **Fact:** Australia has the lowest average purchase frequency (1.52
  orders/customer) among all 8 countries and the second-lowest average
  revenue per customer ($3,471.97), despite a full presence in the
  dataset — a candidate area for retention-focused attention (to be
  formalized with RFM in Phase 8).

## Customer Lifespan (Purchase Span)

* **Fact:** Among customers with 2+ orders, the largest group (**5,318
  customers, 44.74% of all purchasing customers**) has their orders spread
  **over more than a year**. Only 1.46% place repeat orders within a tight
  30-day window, and just 2 customers ever placed multiple orders on the
  same day.
* **Observation:** This is a low-frequency, long-cycle purchase pattern
  typical of electronics retail (durable goods bought infrequently) rather
  than a fast-repeat consumables business — worth keeping in mind when
  interpreting RFM "Recency" thresholds in Phase 8, since what counts as
  "recently active" should reflect this multi-month/multi-year natural
  cadence, not a weekly or monthly one.

## Answers to the Phase 6 Business Questions

> **Is revenue concentrated among a small number of customers?**
> Moderately: top 10% of customers = 35.98% of revenue; top 30% = 69.07%.
> Not an extreme "whale" pattern, but the bottom 50% contributes only
> 13.36% — see `revenue_concentration_deciles` for the full breakdown.

> **What percentage of customers are repeat purchasers?**
> 61.18% (7,272 of 11,887 purchasing customers).

> **Which customer groups generate the majority of revenue?**
> By purchase behavior: repeat customers (82.39% of revenue from 61.18%
> of customers). By geography: United States customers alone (53.58% of
> revenue).
